Outgained opponents by 865 yards. Lost by 186 points.

Kevin Kelley won nine Arkansas titles at Pulaski Academy with an offense that almost never punted. His one season at Presbyterian College (2021) went 2–9. This dashboard tests what the data can and cannot say about why.

Scoring efficiency, not yardage, explains the gap: relative to a combined average of 9.4 points per 100 yards, the +865-yard edge was worth +82 points, offense scoring below average cost 134, and opponents scoring above average cost 134, for −186. That locates the gap; it doesn’t say what caused it.

Fourth-down conversion

Presbyterian converted about one in three attempts. Pulaski’s 2020 rate, the only Pulaski fourth-down figure found so far, was higher and came on fewer tries per game.

Differences are not statistically significant (Fisher exact p = 0.23 Presbyterian vs. its opponents; p = 0.13 Pulaski 2020 vs. Presbyterian). Pulaski 2020 comes from one Democrat-Gazette article and has no 2019 counterpart.

Game by game

Across 11 games, margin correlates −0.50 with attempts (permutation p = 0.12) and +0.42 with conversion rate (p = 0.19). Removing the two wins over non-FCS opponents flips the first to +0.30. Trailing teams take more fourth downs, so these numbers describe game state, not strategy.

What you must believe

Going for it beats punting when a conversion’s gain outweighs a failure’s cost. Let r be the cost of a failed attempt divided by the gain from a conversion; the break-even rate is r ÷ (1 + r). Move the slider to see which observed rates clear the bar.

The ratio changes with field position and distance, and estimating it needs play-by-play data this project doesn’t have yet. Presbyterian’s 34.5% pays off only if a failure costs about half what a conversion gains (r ≤ 0.53); Pulaski’s 47.0% pays off up to r ≈ 0.89.
